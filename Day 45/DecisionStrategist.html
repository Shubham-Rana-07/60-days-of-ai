The Real Decision

You're unemployed, MCA-qualified, aiming to become a Data Scientist by end of 2027 — but freshers aren't getting hired, and that's shaken your confidence in your own degree path. The real trade-off: spend more years and money "restarting" with a BTech, or push forward on the MCA foundation you already have. The Case For Each Option

Option A: Continue on MCA path → Data Scientist

Strongest case: You already hold the degree. No lost years, no extra fees — every month from now can go straight into skills, projects, and applications toward your 2027 goal.

Hidden upside: MCA is often viewed as equal or stronger than BTech for DS/analytics roles in India, since it's more directly aligned to computing.

Biggest weakness: If the real issue is weak portfolio/interview skills, this path doesn't fix that by itself.

Best if you value: speed, momentum, low cost

Option B: Go back and do BTech

Strongest case: Could feel like a "cleaner," more traditionally respected credential if you believe recruiters are filtering on it.

Hidden upside: A structured 4-year program forces consistent skill-building if you struggle with self-discipline.

Biggest weakness: Costs you 3-4+ years and money, blows past your 2027 deadline entirely, and there's no evidence in your own story that degree name is actually the blocker.

Best if you value: perceived prestige, structure, redoing from scratch

Assumption Buster

3 assumptions you may be making:

  • That "companies don't hire freshers" applies equally to you personally, regardless of your portfolio or skills.
  • That a BTech would fix the hiring problem, when you haven't identified the degree as the actual cause.
  • That your MCA is somehow "less than" for data science roles.

2 biases at play:

  • Status quo bias reversed / self-doubt spiral: when the current path feels hard, the untested alternative (BTech) looks falsely safer.
  • Sunk cost avoidance: ironically you're tempted to abandon sunk time in MCA for a fresh start, without evidence it solves anything.

1 thing you're definitely ignoring: You haven't mentioned your actual job-search effort — number of applications, projects, or interview feedback. Without that data, you can't diagnose the real problem.

Premortem — 12 Months From Now

If Option B (BTech) failed...

  • Financial strain from years without income compounds the original problem.

Prevention action: Validate with 5 real recruiters whether BTech vs MCA actually matters before enrolling.
